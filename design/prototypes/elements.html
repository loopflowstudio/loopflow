Three elements, three ways each

The Task row, the workflow line and New Task, each drawn three ways in Old Standard, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Spline Sans Mono. Wine marks what is yours and nothing else. Green marks an agent at work.

The steps are the real feature Graph: explore, plan, build, human-review, deliver, verify. The website's workflow card still shows older step names.

The Task row

It has to answer two questions at a glance: does this need me, and if not, is it moving.

A. Track

Good at
Where each Task is in its workflow. Down a list, the wine segments form a column you can scan.
Costs
The track assumes a straight line; a Task on its third trip through a loop looks the same as its first.

B. Sentence

  • 2 are waiting for you
  • Make Task PR and brief views usefulReview the change. Waiting since 12:07.
  • Keep scrolling responsive under build loadChoose what to build. Waiting since 11:40.

Good at
Needs no legend. Yours sort to the top under a count. The same sentence can be printed by lf task status and read aloud by the phone.
Costs
No picture of progress. Long sentences wrap in a narrow sidebar.

The workflow line

It has to show the order of steps, which ones stop for you, and what repeats.

A. Line with return paths

exploreyou choose
plan
build
human-reviewyou review
deliver
verifyyou try it

back to build until you approve

back to build if verify finds a problem

Good at
One picture: three wine stops, two paths back. Works in the app and on the website.
Costs
Needs about 660px. Nested loops stack downward, so a third level would get tall.

B. Outline

  1. exploreyou choose
  2. plan
  3. Repeat if verify finds a problem
    1. Repeat until you approve
      1. build
      2. human-reviewyou review
    2. deliver
    3. verifyyou try it
Good at
Fits a sidebar or a phone. Nesting is indentation, which everyone already reads. It mirrors how the Graph file is written.
Costs
Tall. Less of a picture, more of a list.

C. Sentence

You choose what to build. The agent plans, then builds until you approve the review. It delivers, and you try it; a problem sends it back to build.

explore* → plan → [ [ build → human-review* ] → deliver → verify* ]
* stops for you   [ ] repeats
Good at
Explains a workflow to someone who has never seen one. The one-line form pastes into a pull request or a terminal unchanged.
Costs
The sentence has to be written per Graph, or generated well. Not scannable across several workflows.

How they combine

These are not exclusive. A is the picture for the Task page and the website, B is the same Graph in a narrow column, and C's one-line form is what lf graph show could print. One Graph, three widths.
