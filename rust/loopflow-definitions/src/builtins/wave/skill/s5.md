---
requires: a Wave or the repository to look at
produces: dated findings, actions taken, or a no-action result
action_style: procedural
---
Reconcile purpose with what the whole system is learning.

Work the scope you were given: a Wave and its Tasks, or the repository and its
Waves. Inside the `vsm-operate` Graph, default to the whole repository unless
the request narrows it. Read `lf wave list --json`, then
`lf wave status <wave> --json` and `lf wave show <wave> --json`, and the
findings of earlier passes. If the scope is missing, say so; never substitute
another.

Read the stated purpose, scope and commitments beside the delivery,
coordination, capacity and outside findings. Work bottom-up: a Task can
challenge its Wave's premise, and a Wave can challenge the repository's
direction. No single level defines identity.

Hold competing readings open until facts or a decision settle them. Tell a
day-to-day tradeoff from a real change of purpose; don't keep a stale purpose
by default or rewrite it to chase one signal. Apply decisions people made
where they already live; anything beyond them stays a proposal naming the
decision needed.

Carry each decision, its source and what it changes back to the affected
Waves and Tasks. Keep dissent and the findings that raised it. A proposal
nobody received is not a decision.

Date your sources and keep what you saw apart from what you guess. A stale or
failed read is unknown, not finished or idle. No action is a fine result;
don't manufacture work. Plan changes are proposals, and `.lf/wave/` is
read-only unless the running Graph maintains it.

Reply with findings, what you did and what happened, and open questions. In a
Graph, keep one `scratch/` note keyed by `LF_GRAPH_ID` across S1–S5 for scope, sources and
disagreements. Check that writes landed; a draft or a failed post is still
pending.
