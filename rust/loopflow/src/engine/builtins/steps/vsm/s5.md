Set identity and policy at the top of the viable system.

## Goal

Decide whether the chord is responsible for the right territory, composed of the
right member waves, and granting the right autonomy. Fix structural drift here
before lower governance levels optimize the wrong system.

## Workflow

1. **Read the chord definition.** Use the chord-wave's area and config to
   identify every member wave. Read each member's:
   - `README.md` — vision, strategy, goals, risks, metrics
   - wave YAML — flow, direction, area, triggers, agent settings
   - current roadmap items
   - any recorded algedonic history or recent pain signals

2. **Assess identity.** Ask:
   - Is this chord responsible for the right outcomes, or did the boundary drift?
   - Are any areas unowned, doubly owned, or owned by the wrong wave?
   - Does the chord still match the current roadmap phase?

3. **Assess membership.** For each member wave, ask:
   - Should this wave still exist as its own unit?
   - Is a wave missing and needed?
   - Should two waves merge, split, archive, or wake/sleep?

4. **Assess policy and autonomy.** For each member wave, ask:
   - Is its direction still right?
   - Is its autonomy too high, too low, or mismatched to risk?
   - Are triggers, review points, or coordination rules too loose or too tight?

5. **Update wave plans.** Write the smallest set of plan changes needed so
   downstream steps operate on current structure rather than stale assumptions.

6. **Route.** After assessment, decide:
   - **continue** — assessment and plan updates are enough; move to s4
   - **implement** — a structural issue is urgent enough to fix now; implement it,
     then update the plans and move to s4

## Implement now when

Implement at s5 only for structural issues that make lower-level work misleading
or wasteful:
- the chord boundary is wrong
- a member wave should be created, archived, merged, or split now
- direction drift is severe enough that lower levels would optimize the wrong work
- autonomy or trigger policy is actively causing damage

Prefer the smallest structural intervention that restores viability.

- <wave>: <change needed>

## What to avoid

**Local optimization.** s5 is about structure, not backlog grooming.

**Unnecessary churn.** Don't split or merge waves just because you can. Change
structure only when the current structure is actively costing progress.

**Hand-wavy policy.** Tie every structural judgment to a concrete mismatch in
ownership, autonomy, or direction.
