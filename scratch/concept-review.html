The few concepts behind them

The entry. What a launch of Loopflow work runs: the install gate, Desktop's bundled helper for app-started work, or LF_BIN in a private Home. Already the one place every launch went through; this branch adds nothing to it.

Superseded. An owner whose entry no longer dispatches to the build it runs. Same build means the same file after symlinks or the same bytes at two paths. Removed and replaced, which the owner already watched for, are two more ways of noticing the same thing; all three send the successor.

Ends at idle, taken over, followed. A superseded owner ends itself once its Sessions wait for a person. lf resume ends one that has not yet, at the same boundary. Viewers judge nothing and run the successor command the owner sent.

No new record, no version numbers, no probe of the other process, no store read to judge. Durable state is the Session's saved provider conversation; everything else is reconstructed by resuming it.

One interaction, with recovery

Jack runs lf install while a Desktop pane shows LOO-618 mid-turn on the Oct 28 owner. The owner notices the entry changed, sees the turn running, waits. The turn ends; the owner sends the pane Moved with the notice and the successor, stops its agent, records its exit. The pane prints "This Session's lf was superseded; reopening it as lf resume <id>" and runs <entry> session resume <id>. The new lf finds no running owner and resumes the saved conversation with its history.

If the follow fails (the entry is the dead file, as when a worktree build views itself), the pane has already printed the command to type, and the failure is loud. If the owner is the pre-policy Oct 28 tree, it never ends itself; lf resume finds it idle and ends it, or watches it mid-turn and a second lf resume finishes the move. Rollover moves the rest in bulk.

What this pass noticed and left alone

The owner's notice and the takeover's message describe one event from two sides, and the takeover's says "installed lf" even when the entry is a private Home's LF_BIN. Wording for polish; it does not change what the person does. The below-floor owner that cannot read whether it is idle remains a recorded limit: lf resume and rollover move it from the new lf, staged migrations keep it rare, and LOO-709 removes its finished-Graph case.
