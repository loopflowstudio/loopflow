Biggest mismatch: the explanation still speaks the retired words

Slice 6 gave the explanation one name for its sync time. The sentences around that field were not read. A person who asks what a Task command would do is told, in the product's own voice, about "planning acquisition" and "Provider freshness": the two words the glossary retires, on the surface whose job is to explain.

The walk. lf --explain task move LOO-1 --wave product on a saved Task prints the report; with no saved Task it fails; with a Wave that isn't registered it fails differently. All three paths end in a retired word.

"Observation only" stays: three explain reports share it and it means the explanation itself, not a tracker event (the plan's scope rule).

Failure and recovery. The failing paths are the ones that carry the words, so they are the ones a confused person reads. Nothing stored or on the wire holds these sentences except the unavailable array of lf --explain --json, which is prose; no reader parses it. Counterexample checked: is "acquisition" a different idea from fetch here, such as taking a lock or a claim? No: each site sits beside fetch_repository / fetch_from_tracker and says that call did not run.

Size. Five strings and one log line; three assertions in rust/loopflow/tests/global_commands.rs (2038, 2053, 2269) and the message at planning_foreground_tests.rs:257; three doc comments that say "planning acquisition" (sqlite/children.rs:156,1499, lf/commands/work_route.rs:68). No type, wire key or SQL.

Larger than this work

  • "action" also names what a command would do. TaskPlanningAction and the "action" key in lf --explain --json task … (create, edit, move, save, comment) predate this change. The glossary already sets Desktop's buttons apart from tracker actions; this is a third sense. Renaming it (change? request?) is a choice about the explain surface, not a leftover.
  • The design doc still narrates with the old verbs. docs/dev/planning.md says "acquire" for fetching at lines 196, 321, 418, 479, 570 and 605, and "observed"/"accepted" throughout its Linear sections. Its glossary is right; its prose predates it. A rewrite of that document, not a rename.
  • "Effect:" in the explanation. The label for what a command would do, beside TaskPlanningAction. Same third sense as the next item.
  • Applying an event is still "accept". accept_linear_issue_in, accept_linear_project_in, project_accepted_planning: about 60 call sites. As a verb for what the store does with an event it reads fine; as the retired noun it doesn't appear. Noted in scratch/questions.md for a follow-up.
  • Two sync times on one Task. tracker_state.synced_at and tracker_synced_at may be one fact. Settling it means reading every writer of both.
  • LinearIssueId holds Asana gids, LinearWritebackState describes any tracker, and Asana's tests live under ops/linear/. A neutral record ID touches every planning signature.
  • Git has no actions. "action" and "lost" on lf repo tracker describe two of three trackers; Git conflicts are a third thing.
  • Columns provider, planning_provider_revision, observed_at, pm_snapshot_synced_at, pm_writeback_json: each a staged rebuild. LOO-960 could carry them with the contract migration.
