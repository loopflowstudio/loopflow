Biggest mismatch: a sentence with no reader

Corrected by the second qa pass (scratch/qa.html): the line is on screen in the view, above the provider's first output, in 8 of 8 lost-thread opens. The first pass's driver deleted it while stripping escapes. This section and the "no reader" item below do not hold; the docs example is back.

open runs twice for a terminal open: once in the process you typed in, which hands off at delegate_session, and once in the held terminal's lf, which goes on to open_waiting_locked and execs the provider. Everything decided after the hand-off is decided where no view exists yet. saved_thread lives there, correctly: it is the one place every opener reaches. Its sentence lives there too, and lands in $LF_HOME/screens/*.ghostsnp.

The same placement holds for "Stopped the … client that had this conversation; continuing here." (TakeOver), which docs/sessions.md also promises with "says so". Not checked in a real terminal here; it is printed on the same side of the hand-off.

The docs claim what a person sees. The line stays where the decision is, so it appears the day a held terminal shows its first view what it printed before the view attached.

Choice made for review. Passed over: printing from the viewing process (a second caller deciding the same thing, and it can disagree with the holder); deleting the line (it is the only trace of why, in the held screen and wherever the server's watcher logs it).

Larger than this work

Lines printed after the hand-off have no reader. Every open-time diagnostic past delegate_session (this one, TakeOver's) goes to a terminal before its first view. One fix covers all of them: a new held terminal shows its first view what is already on its screen. That is held-terminal work and its own Task.

The label. has_provider_history means "recorded". Reading Claude's files for every listed Session is ruled out by the load rule; recording the thread only once the provider saved it would make the projection true and delete thread_lost as an open-time check. Its own Task; touches hook delivery.
