Before → current

The previous script tied scheduling to a checkout and model invocation; any holder of a checkout retained its output. The installed runner now owns scheduling independently and evaluates disposable units. Source lifetime and Task completion do not determine generated-output lifetime.

Accepted and implemented: idle builds retain 4h / 1h below 64 GiB / 15m below 32 GiB. Owner teardown requires at least 24h. Successful disposable evidence retains 24h; failed or unknown evidence retains seven days; saved evidence stays. Held dependencies stay. Native cache caps apply at every pressure level. Accounting labels never authorize deletion.

The consequential mismatch: “cleanup succeeded” does not mean “disk growth is contained.”

The recorded acceptance run removed eligible allocation while free space fell during concurrent work. Keep those measurements separate. Repeated comparable observations must identify recurring growth; unknown coverage and overlapping APFS allocation cannot supply the missing proof.

Failure and recovery

An inactive build loses eligible output and rebuilds later. If an owner cannot be verified or stopped, its output remains and cleanup reports failure. The next run rediscovers candidates under the released lock. A missing or stale receipt cannot establish success.

Source, durable history, saved evidence and private reservations survive. Installed files survive checkout removal. Unmarked demo history stays protected while LOO-725 resolves its runtime model. Existing usage docs and skill instructions need no changes.
