Decide which crash evidence production keeps.

Jack Heart said “lets do it.” The required outcome is Desktop surviving first-paint closes while preserving history, pane moves and Undo. Production reporter policy remains open.

Biggest mismatch: lifetime observation is built; a lifetime repair is not. All 48 comparison trials survived, including immediate release. Later probes found occluded windows without animation callbacks. A failing baseline on an unoccluded display is still missing.

Before → proposed outcome

Existing: pool removal can precede native destruction; diagnostics distinguish both. Native reporting can bypass exit hooks. Proposed, unbuilt: demonstrated safe disposal in the same pool, plus attributable crash evidence. Undo owns recoverability; the pool owns native pages; containers own presentation. No second cache, persisted lifecycle or new user action.

Proposed usage copy: “Close a page, then Undo: its history returns. Moving or hiding a pane keeps its contents. Surviving a close during first paint remains an acceptance target under investigation.”

Failure and recovery: relaunch restores access, not a repair or a promise of live page history after process death. Missing journal/report evidence stays unknown. Failed navigation, WebContent death and owner loss must still permit reclamation; a timeout or paint callback alone proves no safety.

Measured alternatives; production remains unchanged

Disable reporter: the isolated patched Ghostty build produced one signal-journal entry and a matching macOS report after a disposable native fault. Recommended after review and packaging checks.

Keep reporter: synthetic envelope correlation passes its focused checks without reading memory streams. Production integration still needs executable/process-birth launch fields and handling for rotating evidence; native dump memory remains on disk.

Neither alternative repairs WebKit. No experiments need repeating here. Details and private receipt locations are in the plan; dumps and envelopes stay private. No install, merge, upload or OS upgrade.
