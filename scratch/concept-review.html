The few concepts behind them

ConceptMeaning
Entryexisting What a launch runs: the install gate, the Desktop helper, or LF_BIN in a private Home (resolve_lf_binary()).
Supersededproposed An owner whose entry no longer runs its executable. Joins removed and replaced. Judged from files and symlinks; no store read, no version numbers, no probe of the other process.
Ends at idleexisting The lifetime policy: once its Sessions wait for a person, the owner stops its agent and records its exit. lf resume reopens from provider history. Superseded is one more trigger.
Takeoverproposed lf resume never negotiates with an owner on another build; it ends it at the idle boundary and reopens. Same build: attach as today.
Followproposed A viewer told a successor reruns its argv through it. Reverses the "replacement viewers are removed" half of LOO-502; pinning and probing stay removed.

The consequential mismatch, second pass. Three places move an owner, and they do not share a boundary. The owner ends itself only once its Sessions wait for a person. Rollover moves only such owners. lf resume ends a superseded owner whatever it is doing and prints "its last turn was cut off; ask again" (human_session::attaches, then taken_over). The Task's accepted outcome is "moves … at a safe boundary", and Jack's constraint is never to cut a turn silently; being told afterwards is the letter, not the boundary.

proposed One boundary for all three. Idle: end and reopen. Busy and the owner answers its socket: attach, as a same-build open does; the owner ends itself when the turn ends and the viewer follows. Busy and it does not answer: it cannot be watched and cannot be trusted to report idle, so end it and report the cut turn. Rollover gives the same two answers for busy owners. Nothing new is built: answering and owner_waits_for_person exist, and the "cut off" message narrows to the one case nobody can watch. Settled by the accepted design; it iterates to implement.

The consequential mismatch, first pass. The design has one heading, "The one judgement". The code has three. The owner compares paths against the entry's target. lf resume compares content with same_build. follow compares paths again, so a Desktop pane whose bundled lf is byte-identical to the installed CLI still reruns itself through the gate when its owner ends.

One function fixes all three: installation::superseded(executable) = !same_build(executable, entry_target(resolve_lf_binary())), used by the owner, the takeover and rollover's inventory; the viewer's follow reruns the invocation on the owner's word. This is implied by the accepted design, not a new decision. done Implemented 2026-11-02; same_build is private to it and launch_target names the lf a launch runs now. Realign deleted rollover's in_artifact_set, which judged against the published artifact set while the gate dispatches the active selection.

Walking one interaction through, with recovery

Normal path. Jack has a Session open in a cmux pane on 0.15.1. lf install settles 0.15.2 into the gate. Within two seconds the owner sees the gate no longer runs its file. The agent is mid-turn, so nothing happens yet. The turn finishes; the owner sends the pane Moved { notice, successor: gate }, stops the agent, records its exit. The pane prints the notice and reruns its own invocation through the gate, flags and all. The conversation reopens on 0.15.2 with history intact.

The same path from another terminal. While that turn is still running, Jack types lf resume <id> elsewhere. Today the branch ends the owner mid-turn and says so. After this pass it attaches: Jack watches the turn end, the owner ends itself, and this terminal follows the gate too. Only an owner that never answers its socket, the Oct 28 0.15.0 kind, is ended while busy.

Failure. The install also advanced the store past 0.15.1's reader floor. The owner knows it is superseded (that is a file read) but owner_waits_for_person fails (that is a store read). It logs and judges again next tick, forever. The pane sits on the old lf. existing Staged migrations keep old readers working for about 30 days, so this needs an owner older than that.

Recovery. Jack types lf resume <id> anywhere, or runs lf monitor rollover. Both run on the new lf, which reads the store, so both end the owner and reopen. No PID by hand. The deeper fix, if this class recurs: the owner judges idleness from the harness signals it writes itself rather than reading them back. Recorded in the design as a known limit, not scheduled.

What must survive. The saved provider conversation. It does; everything else is rebuilt from it. What deliberately changes. An unviewed idle owner now ends at install instead of at its 24h limit. Counterexample that would invalidate it. Something that needs the idle owner process itself, not its Session. Checked: steers and comments are stored and read at the next turn's context hook; nothing pushes input to an idle owner. An ended Session and an idle one answer a comment identically. Graph runners are never ended; each step launches through the entry.

Questions for Jack

    1. ends, and comes back on the next lf resume or when a pane shows it (branch; "at a minimum when necessary"; lf monitor rollover stays as the "move them all now" sweep), or
    2. comes back live on the new lf right away ("perhaps always"); rollover folds into install and stops being a concept, and rollover must also resume Sessions that ended for supersession to beat the owners' own 2s ending.
  2. The below-floor owner that cannot tell it is idle:
    1. leave it as the recorded limit; lf resume and rollover cover it, or
    2. schedule the owner judging idleness from its own harness signals as the next slice of this Task.
