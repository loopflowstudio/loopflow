Keep automatic recovery; advance the existing design.

No product decision is open. The implemented branch matches the working design; no redesign is proposed.

Actions → concepts → APIs
Open a workspace → one observation of the visible parts → keepWorkCurrent()
Continue through a failure → retained readings and retry delay → failed(at:)
Receive recovered data → continuous health → receive(_:scope:at:)
Close the window → end observation ownership → cancel()

The mismatch this branch resolves

Before: any frame reset the delay. A working part could keep a failing observation reopening every second.

Implemented: failures wait 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then at most 30 seconds. A successful reading starts the healthy interval; known unavailable parts interrupt it until they recover. Checkpoints cannot establish health. Silence after a successful reading counts, so recovery needs no extra timer or traffic.

If one part keeps failing, automatic retries wait progressively longer, even while other parts send updates. The retry delay resets only after the observation stays healthy for about 30 seconds.

One interaction, including recovery

The workspace opens and shows its readings. A subscription ends: the last readings remain marked unavailable while the window reopens its subscriptions. Short successful bursts do not erase repeated failures. Once readings recover and remain healthy for 30 seconds, a later failure retries after one second. Closing the window cancels the observation and interrupts a pending retry.

The delay, healthy start time and unavailable parts belong to this window's retry owner. Existing subscription APIs supply the events. No server, wire-format or storage change is needed; no new action or skill instruction is introduced.

Counterexamples checked against the design

  1. Can Sessions updates hide unavailable planning? No: health tracks each part; another part cannot clear its failure.
  2. Must a quiet workspace emit a frame at 30 seconds? No: failure-time evaluation recognizes the completed healthy interval.
  3. Can a part no longer shown prevent recovery forever? No: the next in-scope reading removes unavailable parts that the scope no longer includes.
