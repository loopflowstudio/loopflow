### Proposed correction (draft, not a new accepted decision)

Admit input and hand its execution responsibility to the owner once, before provider
effects. Admission, reservation and accepted handoff are separate facts on existing
Session/Process/capture records. The creator retains provenance and its own Process
result, but cannot settle handed-off input. Startup acknowledgement means the owner
assumed responsibility, not merely that spawn returned. Resolve missing acknowledgement
against the same reservation without submitting again; owner death preserves uncertainty.

Keep provider ownership stable. The owner checks fresh, revocable viewer input
claims; passive viewing takes no claim. Represent viewer control separately from
AgentProcess attachment. The transaction/record shape and stale creator/viewer
A → B → A fencing still need design. No new job table or parallel history store.

Retain the actual provider channel that received each request. Reconnect snapshots
carry unresolved request identities and an output cursor; answers target the original
request/channel. Unknown delivery stays unknown. Slow viewers cannot block draining;
viewer failure cannot stop the harness, finish capture or close upstream.

The unresolved product choice is the view for an already-running structured
Claude/OpenCode Run: an lf view with original-request answers, or a provider-native
client if that provider proves live attachment. No choice is accepted yet; neither
may silently stop/resume the Run. Existing headless auto-approval remains unchanged
unless Jack Heart selects a different policy. A native terminal chosen at launch
cannot retroactively turn a structured Run into a native TUI.
Native PTY executions can retain their terminal; structured Claude/OpenCode runs
have no native TUI to reattach. A PTY around rendered headless prose supplies no
request-answer channel. Prove Codex native-protocol continuity separately from socket
pathname survival. Flow settlement follows actual execution, and its terminal owner
serializes complete lf OSC reports with step output; provider-only ownership is insufficient.

### Remaining boundary proof

The first executable proof needs all three failures at once: a creator releases
its local capture after accepted handoff; every viewer disconnects during a
pending request; a new viewer answers it. The original input must remain
unsettled until its actual outcome, the provider's native process/thread/turn
must remain unchanged, and neither stale creator nor stale viewer may dispatch
or settle it. Before this passes, process survival alone is not a transport
contract ready for LOO-416. Terminal snapshot library evaluation, local/SSH
attachment, shell retention and complete Flow OSC serialization remain in scope.

Checks: prior `cargo nextest run -p loopflow --lib` filters for attachment A → B → A, relay terminal exit and last-capture drop passed (3 tests, inherited `LF_*` unset, stdin closed); unchanged behavior reuses that evidence. `git diff --check`, `cargo fmt --check` and `lf context`: pass, authored context within both limits; no continuity proof or runtime rerun claimed.

## Accepted scope and delivery

On October 16, 2026, Jack Heart accepted the per-conversation owner architecture
in LOO-416, then requested opening and starting LOO-415 to unblock it. Pursue
through demo/review and publish; do not land or arm merge. The `code` workflow's
`pursue` edge leads to demo; `ship` is not authorized. Research is evidence, not
proof of continuity or direction to absorb adjacent Tasks.

Use an on-demand detached lf owner per conversation, including nested CLI,
headless adapters, native terminals, shells and Flow steps. No shared resident
server or pluggable holder interface. The owner holds transport/lifeline and drains
output with zero viewers; creator, window or Desktop exit after accepted handoff
must not stop execution. Preserve supervision, explicit cancellation, managed
accounts/settings and completed headless Run results. A shell uses an ordinary
recorded Process/pane and retained PTY, not a fake AgentSession.

This supersedes the original brief's multiplexer exemption and assumption that
the Codex engine socket alone provides lifetime continuity. Preserve provider-native
protocols and opaque identities; a structured provider cannot become its native
TUI by replaying text. Evaluate library-backed terminal snapshots if repaint cannot
restore modes/grid/scrollback; do not build a custom emulator or second holder.
Endpoint cost, executable-version handling and exact restoration remain unproved.

Use existing lfdb Session, Process, AgentProcess and capture records with short
transactions and no provider/network I/O under locks. Provider history remains
provider-owned. Keep raw PTY/parser/grid traffic outside SQLite. Passive viewing
acquires no input claim. Missing endpoints and unreachable Machines leave liveness
unknown; they do not authorize another provider or automatic turn/Flow replay.
No stable cross-version interface or older-version support is required.

Remote dispatch uses `lf --machine <label> <command...>`; Jack Heart's October 7
selector decision supersedes historical `lf ssh` examples. The [original brief](evidence/loo-415-original-brief.txt)
retains local/SSH attachment and exact OSC 7501 rules: one owner serializes lf/Flow
reports and step output, provider reports pass through once, and status bytes never
enter JSON/captures/logs or output without a terminal. Preserve working/explicitly
blocked/idle/done/error semantics, lf-only IDs, step clearing and linear-only progress.
The [older remote design](evidence/work-on-another-machine-name.txt) is historical
context. Do not copy competitor code, tests, help or configuration.
