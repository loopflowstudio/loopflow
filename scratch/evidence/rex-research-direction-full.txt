# Session ownership research — October 16, 2026

Jack Heart requested a deep dive into Rex architecture to test whether long-lived
Sessions require an always-running server. Jack added that he has enjoyed the
serverless design space and that theoretically the owners could all be background
processes. Compare a shared on-demand server with detached per-Session processes;
At the initial research request, process ownership was provisional. The later
acceptance below supersedes that status.

Jack also asked which state belongs in lfdb and what interface with agent providers
preserves maximum Session continuity. Evaluate live-turn, PTY/screen/scrollback,
provider conversation/history, unsent input and layout continuity separately;
trace persisted identity, runtime ownership and reconnect capabilities rather than
equating a saved Session ID with survival.

The completed investigation examined Rex primary documentation and Loopflow
ownership. The design-only Flow finished; architecture acceptance followed in
the same conversation. The next requested contribution is the failure audit.

Later direction: Jack accepted the ownership approach and requested an audit of
past loading, stalled selection and forced-restart fixes. Jack wants restarting
to become a safe, pleasant way to choose less context or a fresh conversation,
after reliability is established. Record this downstream intent without expanding
the current reliability work into a context-reset feature.
