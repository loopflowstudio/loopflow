The diff pane
follows the agent.

One idea held fixed: the pane knows which edits the agent made, when, and in answer to what. Four workflows built on it. They differ in when you are looking and in who moves the pane.

The decision: which moment this is for. Watching it work, glancing over while you read, coming back after it stops, or asking why a line is there.

One thing the earlier mock got wrong. It made tool lines in the conversation clickable. The conversation is the provider's own terminal, so Loopflow cannot put links in it. In all four below, everything you click is in the pane; the dark strip above each pane only stands in for the terminal next to it.

1Ride along proposed

While the agent works, the pane shows each edit as it lands and moves to the next one by itself.

For: watching a run you do not yet trust, with your hands off.

Demo
Send a request and look right: each edit appears as its diff the moment it is made. Click the pane and it is yours, with a count of how far ahead the agent has got. Resume rejoins the live edge.
Cost
A served read of the Session's live tool events; the transcript readers in loopflow-harness already parse them for four harnesses, and an edit call carries its own before and after. Plus a follow mode on TaskFilesStore.
Risk
Nobody sits and watches for long. A fast agent strobes the pane, and it contradicts what is built: live updates never move your reading anchor.
Followingidle

Press Play, then click in here while it runs.

2Presence proposed

The pane stays where you put it. The agent shows up in it the way a collaborator's cursor does: its edits arrive marked, and a chip says where it is working.

For: reading the change while the agent is still writing it.

Demo
You are reading hunk 2. A hunk further down gains a mark and just now; the chip reads Agent is in ReviewStreamView.swift L51 · Go. Nothing scrolls unless you press Go. When the turn ends, the marks stay as this turn until the next one.
Cost
The same live read as 1, with no follow mode: a mark on rows the stream already draws, and one chip. The smallest of the four, and it keeps the anchor promise as built.
Risk
Quiet enough to miss. It tells you where the agent is, never why.

3Replay the turn proposed

When the agent stops, the pane can walk you through what that turn did: its edits in the order it made them, each under the sentence it wrote before making it.

For: coming back after you looked away, which is most of the time.

Demo
The turn ends and Changes offers Turn 13 · 4 edits · 2 min. ] steps edit by edit; above each diff is what the agent said it was about to do, and the walk ends on what it ran and what that printed. Leave it and you are back in the ordinary stream.
Cost
No live plumbing: a finished transcript has the edits, their order and the text between them. One served read per turn and a reader mode on the existing rows and stepper. Works for a closed Session and for a Graph step nobody watched.
Risk
It shows edits, not the result: a line edited three times in one turn appears three times. Changes made by a shell command (cargo fmt, a codegen script) are in no edit call; the replay has to say "2 files also changed outside edits".
Turn 13 · 4 edits · 2 min · you: "make Files' Diff step like Changes does"

4Every line knows its turn radical success

Any change in the pane can answer "why is this here": which request it came from, what the agent said when it wrote it, and which pass of the Graph touched it last. The diff and the conversation become two views of one history.

For: review, days later, when the conversation has scrolled away or was never yours to see.

Demo
Each hunk carries a small turn mark. Click one: Turn 13 · 12:31 · you asked "make Files' Diff step like Changes does", the agent's sentence, and Only this turn. Filter the whole stream to one turn, or to one Graph step: what did compress change that implement wrote?
Cost
The largest. Attributing the lines of the final diff to turns means either a checkout snapshot at each turn boundary or replaying every edit in order, on the Rust side, carried on the lf task diff DTO. 1, 2 and 3 fall out of it nearly free once it exists.
Risk
A wrong answer to "why" is worse than none. Lines written by shell commands have no turn; they must read unattributed, never a guess. A Task's history spans several Sessions and harnesses.
