Combined direction

One library. Room to read.

Choose a scope on the left, scan compact rows, and read the selected brief alongside them. Working Task shortcuts go directly to their ongoing conversations. The repository conversation is a named, separate destination.

Try editing LOO-452, reading related LOO-387, visiting Repository conversation, and returning. The draft stays with LOO-452. Drag a row to reorder the sample. Select another Task to replace the reader; each Task keeps its draft. There are no brief history controls or tabs.

What stays together

Planning workspace → scope + compact collection + inspected brief
Repository conversation → its own full-width destination → return to retained planning
Working Task → its own existing conversation → return to retained planning

“Composed” here joins the library and its reading context. The repository terminal occupies a separate destination. This combines the three answers without squeezing a conversation alongside the list and brief.

An inspected brief can remain open when you change a filter. Its membership stays visible, and the reader explains when it sits outside the current collection. Following a related Task changes the reader; it does not refile work or silently broaden the list.

On narrow screens the authored layout stacks collection and reader. The native design must keep both reachable without shrinking terminal input. Real scroll retention, layout and input continuity remain unverified.

Why Obsidian suggests more than a graph

Its useful contribution here is the relationship between a collection and the documents being read. A retained list, pinned context and a short reading trail can keep several ideas available. A graph would need meaningful Task relationships first; proximity or shared words cannot supply them.

Heptabase’s canvas is another access path to shared content. Loopflow already has Dashboards and retained panes. This exploration borrows context retention without proposing another canvas or planning store.
