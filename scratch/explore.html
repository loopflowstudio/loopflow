Three proposed experiences for starting useful work, reviewing it in the same conversation, and knowing what happens next. Try the controls, then choose a direction—or combine them.

All examples below are simulated. Buttons change this page only. Nothing starts, publishes, merges, or records a choice in Loopflow.

Built from the kickoff design, TaskWorkView.swift, NewTaskSheet.swift, task_graph.rs, the builtin feature / deliver graphs, and LOO-733’s review-panel design. The example “Export filtered results” and its checks are fictional.
01 / ROUTE

A. Follow the route

You can see the entire journey before taking the next step.

Appeal: reviews, execution and iterations have one visible home. Cost: closest to today’s workspace; shared move descriptions and one live diagram. Risk: a newcomer still has a map to learn before getting useful work done.

02 / NEXT DECISION

B. Work the next decision

The work you’re reviewing stays in front of you. The next move explains itself.

Appeal: useful at a glance; the same conversation carries each round. Cost: shared moves plus a compact action area; integrates with LOO-733’s panel without choosing its layout. Risk: the larger plan is easier to miss, so the next stop and merge effect must always be visible.

03 / REPEATABLE DELIVERY

C. Start from something that worked

A verified delivery is the beginning of the next one.

Appeal: workflows become useful examples you can inspect, reuse and improve. Cost: largest—connect historical instructions, checks and delivery to today’s editable definitions. Risk: old success can look like a promise; the new Task must earn its own checks and approval.
