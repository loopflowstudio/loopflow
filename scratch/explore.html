Held fixed from Round 1: Base order is B · Start-Time Stack — newest-started Tasks land at the top (⌘1), manual drags hold in task_orders, and entering a Task or sending a prompt never jumps it.
Open in Round 2: (1) What does the sort button sort by — terminal status (like Rex) or Graph step? (2) How do we get hands-off Tasks out of the way — smart “heading to end” sink, explicit Close/Park, or both?

What counts as “moving towards the end” (even without Auto)?

Loopflow already knows each Task's Graph step, live Graph process, PR merge state, and auto flag. Two clean definitions to feel below:

  • Heading to done (narrow): auto is on (and not blocked), or a live Graph is in deliver (past ship-review), or its PR has auto-merge armed / CI settling on GitHub. Tasks in plan or build stay in your active stack.
  • Autonomous run (broad): Any Task with a live Graph moving on its own (plan, build, or deliver) or PR settling — it sinks while churning and pops back when it reaches ship-review, verify, or blocks.

B1 · Rex-Style Sort Button (by Terminal Status)

Proposed

Start-Time Stack stays dead calm on its own; clicking one button (⇅ Status) snapshots Blocked → Available → Idle → Busy into task_orders once, then freezes again.

Demo

Work across 6 Tasks: sending prompts and receiving replies never moves a single row. When you finish a stretch and want to sweep waiting agents, click ⇅ Status in the sidebar header (or shortcut). Blocked and Available Tasks snap to the top once; when you click ⌘1 and hit Enter, it stays right at ⌘1.

Appeal

  • Zero surprise motion — sorting only happens at the exact moment you press the button.
  • Familiar mental model from Rex: calm list between sweeps, one-click triage when you want it.
  • Works identically for Graph Tasks and ad-hoc chat Tasks (every Task has session status).

Cost & risk

  • Cost: Small (~1 day past PR #2025). Add started_at default in work_in, plus a one-shot sort command/button that writes the sorted IDs to task_orders. Zero migration.
  • Overwrites drags: pressing Sort replaces any manual drag arrangement you made earlier.
  • Doesn't move quiet Auto / finishing Tasks out of the way on its own unless paired with B3 or B4.

B2 · Sort by Graph Step

Proposed

Each row shows its Graph step; one click on ⇅ Step orders your stack by pipeline progress — either finish-line first (verify → ship-review → deliver → build → plan) or interactive stops first.

Demo

Advance a Task from build to ship-review (or deliver to verify) using the simulator controls, then click ⇅ Step. Compare Closest to done (finish nearly-done work first) against Needs review first (verify & ship-review on top, build/plan middle, deliver at bottom). Between clicks, nothing moves.

Appeal

  • Matches Loopflow's core work model: clearing verify and ship-review unblocks merges faster than starting new plans.
  • Shows where every Task actually is in its Graph without clicking into each one.
  • One-shot button means advancing a step never yanks a row while you're in it.

Cost & risk

  • Cost: Small–Medium (1–1.5 days). Batch-read task_graphs + live graph_processes in work_in (no migration) and carry the step label on sidebar rows.
  • Non-code Tasks: ad-hoc chat Tasks (no Graph) and custom Graphs need a fallback rule (progress fraction + interactive flag, with chat Tasks grouped at top or bottom).
  • A Task can be Blocked on a question during build — step order alone won't float it above an unblocked ship-review.

B3 · Start-Time Stack + Smart “Heading to End” Sink

Proposed

Tasks advancing toward the finish line without you — explicit Auto, or running deliver, or PR settling — automatically tuck below a hairline and pop back up if they block or reach review.

Demo

Select LOO-1042 at ship-review (with Auto off) and click Advance step → into deliver. Even though Auto isn't set, Loopflow sees it is heading to the end and glides it under Heading to done at the bottom. Advance once more to verify (or click Block / fail CI): it pops right back into your active stack. Toggle Narrow vs. Broad above the list to feel whether plan/build runs should sink too.

Appeal

  • Solves the “finished my part, now it's just landing” clutter with zero manual bookkeeping.
  • Smart predicate catches non-Auto Tasks in deliver or waiting on GitHub auto-merge — not just the literal Auto flag.
  • Typing in a normal Task (Available → Busy) still never moves it; only Graph-level autonomous progress sinks.

Cost & risk

  • Cost: Medium (1.5–2 days). Zero schema migration: work_in joins existing task_graphs, task_prs (merge mode / CI), and auto to compute one boolean predicate (heading_to_end) carried on the wire for Desktop & iOS.
  • Broad vs. narrow edge: if plan/build sink too, kicking off a brand-new Task immediately sends it to the bottom — can feel like the old jump unless limited to post-review / Auto.
  • Doesn't let you manually hide an idle Task you're deliberately ignoring for a few hours.

B4 · Clean Desk: Tidy Button + Smart Sink + Close (Park)

Proposed · Radical

Start-Time Stack at rest, a one-shot Tidy control (Status or Step), smart auto-sink for Tasks heading to the end, and a tab-like × Close to park any Task into the bottom drawer without abandoning it.

Demo

Start with 6 Tasks: two heading to done (Auto in build and a non-Auto Task in deliver) already sit quietly in the bottom drawer. Click × on an idle Task you won't touch until tomorrow — it tucks into the drawer (still Working, still running, never abandoned). Click ⇅ Status or ⇅ Step to tidy your remaining active stack. If a parked or heading-to-done Task hits verify or blocks, it pops right back into your active stack automatically.

Appeal

  • Combines machine judgment (heading to done) with human judgment (“hide this for now”) in one place.
  • Gives you both Rex's one-shot Status sort and Graph-step sort without ever reordering behind your back.
  • Keeps your visible sidebar at the 3–5 Tasks you're actually steering even when 15 are open.

Cost & risk

  • Cost: Medium–Large (2.5–3.5 days). Needs a Machine-local parked_tasks table (additive migration) beside task_orders, the shared heading_to_end predicate in Rust, one-shot Tidy actions, and the bottom drawer in Desktop & iOS.
  • Naming hazard: “Close” can be misread as Complete or Abandon — needs unmistakable verb/copy (e.g. Park or Hide, tooltip “Keep running, tuck below”).
  • Auto-reopen tuning: a manually parked Task should pop back on Blocked / interactive review, or stay parked until you click it? (See Q4.)
