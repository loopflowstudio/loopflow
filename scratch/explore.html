Opening a brief

The side pane is out. How should a brief open instead? Four ways to try on the real backlog, each a button below.

Everything else from round 6 stays: compact rows, one compact context line, reordering, the repository conversation as its own destination. Only the reader changes. The round 6 page is here; the six options are here.

Tick rows to select several; Start and Claim then act on the selection, whichever way briefs open. All 46 unstarted Product Tasks with their full briefs, from the November 14 snapshot. Edits and order live in this page only. Briefs run from 291 to 8,076 characters, so try a short one (LOO-787) and the longest (LOO-879, last row).

What each one is

What is trueGood atCosts
a · Expand in placeA row opens under its own title and stays open. Open as many as you like.Comparing two or three briefs. Neighbors never move away.Three long briefs open and the list is gone; you scroll a wall. Position in the order gets hard to see.
b · One cardOne Task at a time lifts into a card where it sits. The rest dims. Arrow keys walk the card down the list.Going through the backlog one by one. Always clear what is selected and where it ranks.No side-by-side. A long brief makes a very tall card.
c · Take the pageThe brief replaces the list at full width, with previous and next in a strip. Esc returns to the exact row.Reading and editing long briefs properly. The most room for words.The list is hidden while you read. It is a destination, so it is one step further from “just glancing”.
d · Summary, then allFirst click shows the opening paragraph and the brief's section headings. “Read all” opens the rest in place.Judging many briefs fast without a wall of text. Long briefs stay manageable.Two steps to read anything fully. The first paragraph is often provenance (“Follow-up to LOO-353…”), not the point.

Native cost is close for all four: each replaces the reader pane in the preview with list-row state. c needs scroll position kept across the switch, which the round 6 design already required for the conversation destination.

Questions

  1. How does a brief open?
    • 1a Expand in place, several at once
    • 1b One card at a time
    • 1c Take the page
    • 1d Summary, then all
    A mix is an answer: “1b, and Return opens 1c”.
  2. What about the long ones?
    • 2a Open to full height whatever the length
    • 2b Cap the height, with “Show all”
    • 2c Short briefs open in the list; long ones take the page
  3. Where do Edit, Start and Move live?
    • 3a Inside the opened brief, as sketched
    • 3b On every row, shown on hover
    • 3c Keys and a context menu only; the list stays clean
