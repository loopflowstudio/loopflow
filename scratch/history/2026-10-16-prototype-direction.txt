# Navigation prototype direction

October 16, 2026 — Jack Heart requested three prototypes exploring different
interpretations of the navigation vision, with kickoff preceding the studies.

The October 16 navigation brief remains the accepted direction. Explore distinct
interaction and presentation choices within it, preserving Work ownership,
one primary-or-explicitly-zoomed pane per tile, query-only membership, retained
Task layouts, contextual sidebar and title-bar navigation. Preserve earlier
studies as evidence. Compare all three through the same repo → Wave → Task →
Wave scenario; prototypes do not establish native retention or performance.

Jack Heart then requested breaking the exploration into specific subproblems,
solved one by one, so prototypes do not conflate a mixed bag of design decisions.

Jack Heart selected **Navigation and returning to your place** for the first
comparison. This supersedes the initial suggestion to start with tile geometry.
Keep sample Tasks, Wave grid geometry, typography, colors and pane content fixed.
Compare three navigation mechanisms: hierarchy through breadcrumbs; an explicit
return trail to the exact originating view; and an explicit Open alongside action
retaining the Wave next to the Task. All preserve the required breadcrumbs.
Each exercises opening a Task, retaining a draft, visiting another Task and
returning. Open alongside is a selection operation on owned Work, not a new
unowned workspace or duplicated native surface.

Subsequent questions, after reviewing the first comparison: Wave tile geometry;
then touched arrangements, arrivals/departures and inbox placement. Query
editing/pinning and chrome can receive separate studies as needed.
These are proposed study boundaries, not accepted production choices.

Jack Heart subsequently authorized a UX-independent technical foundation while
away and multiple prototype sets for later review. The scoped pane foundation is
implemented; nine alternatives cover navigation, tile geometry and arrivals.
See [studies](arrange-studies/README.md) and [foundation result](foundation-result.md).
This supersedes the earlier production hold only for the scoped foundation;
remaining UX and landing stay at their review boundaries.

Jack Heart named the pinned cross-Wave view **Available**, replacing “Needs you”.


## Selected combination

Jack Heart selected **01 A (Breadcrumbs), 02 B (Attention first), 03 A (At the
end)** and asked to continue. These three choices are accepted. The combined
study is now the default entry; all nine isolated alternatives remain accessible.
Breadcrumbs follow hierarchy; Back retains query/visit history. Attention gives
waiting Tasks more space until the first arrangement edit, which freezes both
order and proportions. New matches then append at the end. Query syntax/pinning,
native titlebar/input and shared-model integration remain separate work.
