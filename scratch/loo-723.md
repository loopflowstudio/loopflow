## Decisions and constraints

The implementation remains a headless design pending review. CLI-only omits
first-time Desktop setup; an existing release app participates in every update.
Raw promotion may reuse that app only when its helper matches the candidate;
`lf install` acquires the complete signed bundle. No helper-routing fallback,
piecemeal signed-bundle replacement, schema change or private-dev selection change.

The November 4 concept review required updates to retain the app reached by the
public CLI without the original shell environment. Explicit `LF_APPLICATIONS_DIR`
wins; otherwise use that linked bundle, then `/Applications` for initial setup.
This follows Jack Heart's coherence/custom-location outcome, not a separately
approved design. Acquisition, freshness and promotion carry one resolved target;
receipt recovery retains its pinned target before a new destination is selected.

Receipt-backed promotion remains the activation owner. Matching files still
reconcile receipts/gates before success. Only Desktop is quiesced/reopened;
Session owners and Graphs keep their images. Renamed bundles remain while used;
existing idle retirement owns eligible turnover. Preserve rollback bytes, native
identities, pending input and recovery paths.

Jack Heart's November 4 steer assigns immediate local Desktop repair to the
parent; permanent installation policy and mini rollout remain here. LOO-724 owns
retained hooks and reconnect/input recovery, and LOO-722 owns cleanup. Neither
sibling repair nor parallel cleanup/schedule changes belong to this implementation.
The accepted both-Machine verification requirement remains.

**Open product choice: may a scheduled update close an open Desktop?** The branch
currently treats shell and scheduled installation alike: `quiesce_app_processes`
sends SIGTERM to Desktop and escalates to SIGKILL after five seconds, then
`resume_switch_app` reopens it. Excluding Session helpers from those signals is
necessary but does not establish preservation of the visible workspace, unsubmitted
input or usable connections. Immediate repair authorization does not decide the
routine unattended-update experience.

- **1a — restart automatically (current branch):** the schedule activates the
  complete update and reopens Desktop. Updates arrive while Desktop stays in use;
  visible interruption and restoration need explicit acceptance and proof.
- **1b — defer scheduled activation (proposed, recommended):** while Desktop is
  open, keep the entire existing selection and retry at the next scheduled run.
  An explicit `lf install` still updates immediately and may reopen Desktop.
  This avoids an unattended interruption but can delay updates indefinitely on
  a Machine that keeps Desktop open. No persistent staged-update lifecycle is
  proposed. Jack Heart has not selected either option in this review.

Proposed usage wording for 1b: “Scheduled updates leave an open Desktop alone and
retry on the next run. Run `lf install` to update now; it may close and reopen
Desktop. The app and CLI always update together. Running conversations retain
their identities and work.” The final sentence is required acceptance, not a
claim of verified configured continuity. Under 1a, replace the first two sentences
with “Scheduled updates may close and reopen Desktop to install the complete
update.” Keep the existing pairing, custom-location and CLI-only guidance.

Proposed skill guidance: “Before rollout, preserve evidence of an existing
conversation and its pending input. Verify reconnect, a completed turn and continued
interaction afterwards. A surviving helper PID or a reopened window is insufficient.”
For 1b, make scheduled intent explicit at entry and decide deferral before creating
an install switch or advancing the store. Retain one acquisition/promotion owner;
explicit updates and receipt recovery keep their existing paths. Recovery of an
already-started switch must finish its pinned target before considering deferral
of a new update. Never defer only the app while advancing its CLI or store.

**Source finding independent of 1a/1b:** Swift `AppUpdates.isInstalled` still
recognizes only `/Applications` or `LF_APPLICATIONS_DIR`. Both `AppUpdater.start`
and `adoptIfUpdated` use it. A custom installed app launched normally without that
environment is therefore excluded from its Sparkle/adoption path even though Rust
now follows the public CLI link. This is a source counterexample, not an installed
reproduction. Make Desktop use the same installation identity/location facts while
continuing to exclude private builds; do not add another destination registry.
The Rust `adopt` repair alone does not repair Swift's gate before that call.

Next proof: custom installed app launch without the original environment enables
the appropriate update/adoption path, private dev launch stays excluded, and a
scheduled candidate arriving during an open conversation follows the selected
restart policy. Preserve draft, Session/provider identity and sustained input
across explicit activation and interrupted receipt recovery. Acquisition failure
leaves the old selection; recovery cannot silently change the target. The signed
desktop/mini and full disposable-account recovery obligations remain unchanged.
