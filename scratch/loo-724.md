## Implemented recovery and remaining acceptance

`confirmed_group_death` releases any local lifeline, then observes group death
for up to three seconds regardless of writer ownership. Empty groups return
immediately; persistent survivors retain the exact diagnostic and no signal is
sent. The Session attachment lock still fences observation and replacement.

Two `session_record::runtime` regressions start a headless leader and helper,
observe the leader's exit, then recover through `resume_session_agent_process`
in a separate test process. A pipe-controlled helper exits naturally during the
observation window; a persistent helper remains alive and refuses replacement.
They preserve the Session, capture, Task, native identity, history and pending
steer. Successful recovery reserves a fresh AgentProcess/token and rejects stale
settlement; refusal preserves the prior attachment and Process record. This
proves the recovery entry, not an installed provider turn or released wire upgrade.

Source inspected at `cedb7b52b6` includes upstream #1800's framed viewer stream,
#1804's idle lifetime policy and #1809's native-status restoration:

- `transport/lifetime.rs` judges executable changes and protected work, then sends
  `control.successor = [entry, session, resume, id]`. Store failures retry; they
  cannot establish idle. The viewer follows that command, but socket EOF fails
  with unknown outcome and no automatic resume.
- `human_session::attaches` checks exact owner evidence and compares its image
  to `installation::launch_target`. An answering busy superseded owner is viewed;
  an idle or unanswering superseded owner is ended before native continuation.
  `viewed_summary` probes control instead of trusting stale viewer records.
- `open_waiting` takes the launch lock and `resume_native_session` uses saved
  provider identity. Agent settlement retains fencing and rejects unknown live
  identity. Missing native history can select a fresh launch, so the affected
  Sessions' saved identity must be established before recovery.
- `resolve_lf_binary` prefers the invoking app's helper outside a private Home;
  `superseded` compares against that resolved entry, not merely the public CLI.
  Mixed app/CLI installations therefore matter to both callbacks and takeover.

These are mechanisms, not a diagnosis of Jack's reported incident. Remaining:

1. Identify affected old Sessions and capture their Session/native/capture/Work
   identities, owner and AgentProcess receipts, entry paths and concrete failed
   reconnect diagnostics. Distinguish store refusal, owner loss, wire rejection,
   unknown attachment identity and stale UI state from observed evidence.
   Trace input delivery, attachment fencing, terminal ownership and provider exit
   after handoff against the reopened-conversation receipt and Jack's later report.
2. Extend behavioral proof to an already-running Session across owner/build
   replacement and viewer return, including the relevant old wire/store frontier.
   Assert the same Session/native history/Work, fresh fencing only when ownership
   changes, preserved pending work and no replay. Existing stale-viewer,
   unanswering-owner and successor-command fixtures are useful partial coverage.
   The separate-process runtime regressions now cover the helper teardown gap;
   existing transport fixtures cover owner replacement and native launch separately.
3. After coherent supported installation and coordination with the parent,
   restore the identified affected Sessions. Verify accepted input, a completed
   turn and stable continued interaction with preserved identity/history/drafts;
   a visible prompt or live process is insufficient. Re-fire retained hooks.
   The auto Graph's landing endpoint does not itself
   satisfy this installed outcome; recovery remains required for Task completion.
