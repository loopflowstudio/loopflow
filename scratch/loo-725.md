## Current implementation

- `PendingInput` retains queued bytes and suppresses writable-PTY polling until
  a 100 ms retry deadline. Retention attempts never wait on the store mutex or
  SQLite writer lock; other writers keep their original busy timeout. Changed
  failures log once; recovery resumes record-before-delivery. Poll deadlines round
  up to milliseconds.
- `owner_retention` replaces the boolean-only judgment across retirement,
  resume and rollover. Named facts distinguish native input, Graph, headless
  Run, live turn and missing Session. The watcher also reports output/store
  retention, reuses its connection and limits policy work to the two-second
  poll regardless of activity. Ending-confirmation errors share deduplication.
- Host-security pressure stops later verification suites and retains `not_run`
  receipts. Ordinary product failures still allow independent suites to run.
- `demo.md` requires a complete copied runtime in a private root outside build
  output, with private `LF_HOME` and copied `LF_BIN` for launches, child commands
  and teardown. It records exact owners and retains the root/history until
  verified teardown. These are lifecycle instructions; configured proof remains.

The blocking writer, duplicate transport queue and boolean-only retention
judgment are removed. `PendingInput` retains the recorded-byte count for partial
writes, one retry deadline and the previous error; policy operations keep
separate error histories so unrelated successes cannot cause repeated warnings.

## Demo cleanup decision — open, 2026-11-04

The experience is: return to the same editable conversation after a rebuild,
then finish the demo without broken owners or lost history. A Session retains
conversation identity; its live owner retains the editor and pending requests.
Directory placement establishes neither a Session lifecycle nor disposability.

| Action | Concept and current API | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type while the store is busy | `PendingInput` → `try_record_owner_input` → PTY | Queue until retention commits; keep control responsive. |
| Detach, rebuild, return | Session → `lf resume SESSION`; `owner_retention` protects its owner | Same editable input/request; copied runtime and private store stay valid. |
| Finish the demo | Verified owner → existing Session/process stop controls | Confirm exit before reclaiming selected runtime files; preserve durable history. |
| Resume after teardown | Same Session and provider history → `lf resume SESSION` in its private Machine | Reopen saved history without replay; this cannot recreate a dead native editor. |

Retention reasons and security-pressure receipts describe existing decisions;
they grant no deletion authority. No new public state, DTO, schema, retry command,
scheduler or automatic provider restart is needed.

`demo.md` puts the private Machine, repository and provider history inside the
runtime root. LOO-722's `scripts/disk_cleanup.py` selects roots under
`.lf-machine/demos` whose `.disposable-coding-demo` marker says
`disposable runtime; evidence stored elsewhere`. Its deletion unit is the root.
Marking the current layout would authorize deleting durable history; leaving it
unmarked supplies no automatic reclamation. Idle owners and closed viewers do
not settle eligibility.

**Demo/review must choose:** separate durable Machine/provider history from
disposable runtime so LOO-722 can reclaim eligible output, or retain complete
unmarked fixtures until explicit teardown. Separation also needs a completion
action that settles whether a still-editable draft or pending request is
disposable. Stop-before-delete applies after eligibility; `9ad44189` preserves
history and does not settle this choice.

If separation is selected, the proposed skill change keeps durable history
outside the disposable root, records same-Session reopening, and marks only
eligible runtime after recording completion disposition. No archival mechanism
or approval is implied. The current safe assumption keeps the unmarked fixture
intact until explicit teardown, with no marker, expiry or competing cleaner.
This does not establish Jack's acceptance of indefinite retention.

Preservation counterexamples: stopping an owner loses its unsaved editor even
when history is copied; screenshots are not resumable history; moving Machine
or provider directories can invalidate recorded absolute paths; interrupted
teardown must leave surviving verified owners' files intact. The chosen layout
