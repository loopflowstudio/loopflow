## Current implementation

- `PendingInput` retains queued bytes and suppresses writable-PTY polling until
  a 100 ms retry deadline. Retention attempts never wait on the store mutex or
  SQLite writer lock; other writers keep their original busy timeout. Changed
  failures log once; recovery resumes record-before-delivery. Poll deadlines round
  up to milliseconds.
- `owner_retention` replaces the boolean-only judgment across retirement,
  resume and rollover. Named facts distinguish native input, Graph, headless
  Run, live turn and missing Session. The watcher also reports output/store
  retention, reuses its connection and limits policy work to the two-second
  poll regardless of activity. Ending-confirmation errors share deduplication.
- Host-security pressure stops later verification suites and retains `not_run`
  receipts. Ordinary product failures still allow independent suites to run.
- `demo.md` creates a durable private root (Machine, provider history, fixture
  repository) and a separate runtime root (copied runtime, generated output) before
  launch, with private `LF_HOME` and copied `LF_BIN` for launches, child commands and
  teardown. The person's settled/given-up answer at review's end is the release;
  settled input allows verified teardown, given-up input leaves owners running.
  `.lf/skills/disk-cleanup.md` names `~/.lf-machine/demos/<id>` and the marker. These
  are lifecycle instructions; configured proof remains.

## Demo cleanup decision — settled by accepted decisions, 2026-11-04

The experience is to return to the same editable conversation after a rebuild,
then finish the demo without broken owners or lost history. Session identity
persists; its live owner holds the editor and pending requests. `PendingInput`
is transport machinery, not a new public conversation state.

| Action | Current API and required result |
| --- | --- |
| Type while storage is busy | `PendingInput` → `try_record_owner_input` → PTY: queue until retention commits; keep control responsive. |
| Detach, rebuild, return | `lf resume SESSION` and `owner_retention`: preserve the same editor/request and valid private runtime/store. |
| Finish the demo | Record each draft/request, release the runtime root; existing Session/process stop controls confirm verified owners exited before it is reclaimed. |
| Resume after teardown | `lf resume SESSION` in the durable private Machine: reopen the same saved history without replay; this cannot recreate a dead editor. |

Earlier reviews held two choices open for demo/review. Jack Heart's own statements
answer both; earlier passes read `2693d72d` for its stop-before-delete policy and
missed what it excludes. This is a reading of his decisions, not a new decision by
him, and his demo review can overturn it.

1. **History lives outside the disposable runtime.** His accepted brief prefers
   stopping owners and reclaiming disposable demo directories "over indefinite
   retention", preserves durable history, and asks for clean restart from retained
   history after cleanup. Retaining the whole root until teardown cannot meet these
   together: "neither permits deleting history" makes that root permanent.
2. **Unsettled input cannot hold a released root.** `2693d72d`: "Pending drafts or
   quiet-but-live demo owners must not silently retain eligible disposable output
   forever." `demo.md`'s "Retained or unresolved input keeps its owner and complete
   fixture" contradicts it, and so does this plan's former "until disposition".
   Input is recorded and may be lost with its owner. Saved history survives; a dead
   editor does not. Waiting for settlement is not available as a default.

Limits that remain in force: LOO-709's protection still governs idle, replaced and
superseded owners, so no lifetime, resume or rollover code changes. Only authorized
cleanup of a released root ends a draft-holding owner. `4f0b98a7` and `9ad44189` keep
the 24h minimum, so the demo itself never terminates an owner holding unsettled
input: it records the input and releases the root.

Marker: it stays out of the builtin because LOO-722's runner is machine-local and
generic demos do not auto-enroll. Writing it at release restarts the cleaner's
newest-write window, which supplies the 24h minimum; an unknown outcome gets seven
days. Marking at creation would let a long live review age out. `.lf/directions/` was
rejected as its home: prompt directions were removed and nothing reads that directory.

Abandoned review: nothing releases the root. Its runtime stays valid and the cleaner
reports it retained, so it is visible and is not the incident's path-loss condition.
Unresolved for demo review: should an abandoned review release itself, and at what
age? No accepted decision says. Reclamation waits on it; source delivery does not.

Preservation counterexamples: killing an owner loses its editor/request even with
copied history; screenshots are not resumable history; moving Machine/provider
directories can invalidate absolute paths, which is why the split precedes launch.
Interrupted teardown must keep files of surviving verified owners.

The configured walkthrough must cover typing, detach/rebuild/resume, release with
settled and unsettled input, private launch/child/teardown routing, interrupted
cleanup, final death recheck and same-history reopening. Store failure must queue
input without blocking control, then deliver once on recovery; eligible teardown
can retry after surviving owners exit.

Publication: delivering the source marks and deletes nothing. Merge leaves combined
cleanup proof and installed acceptance open, and reaching `end` requests completion
without establishing either. Review page: `scratch/concept-review.html`.
