## No open Session at resume — concept review, 2026-11-04

`OwnerRetention` mixes two things. Native input, Graph, Run and live turn are work.
`NoOpenSession` (formerly `MissingSession`) is an absence of evidence: no open Session
names the owner. The row may exist and be merely complete, and at `attaches` that is
always so, because the caller holds the Session it is opening.

`attaches` once protected every reason alike, so this branch and #1818 changed one case
without a decision. A live owner of a completed Session that did not answer its socket
failed `lf resume` forever; before #1818 it ended and the conversation moved. The transport
README's own contract lists drafts, requests, turns, Graphs and Runs, not this.
Jack Heart's October 26 decision is that `lf resume` always gets you back, and the only
accepted exception is work saved history cannot restore. The change is in neither
`unanswering_superseded_owners_preserve_work` fixture.

The case is live. PID 29478 is an lf 0.14.19 owner, parent 1, up 7d21h, on a superseded
hashed binary in `~/src/etude`. Its Session "Reduce machine load" completed 2026-10-28
00:19 UTC and the owner started at 01:09: the reopened-retired case, on a real Machine.
It is the only one of 73 ledger-live owners with Sessions (read-only query; only this
PID was checked with `ps`). The `completed_at` repair covers future binds, not it, and
nothing shows whether it answers. It was not touched.

Decided and built: `NoOpenSession` displays "no open Session" and is documented as
unknown, not work. The watcher still retains on it: a truly absent row must not
establish eligibility, and a recreated empty store can produce one. In `attaches`, work
reasons return true; `NoOpenSession` keeps main's behavior: an answering owner is
attached; an unanswering superseded owner is ended and the conversation moves, as an
idle one is; otherwise `answering` decides.

Rejected: ending an answering `NoOpenSession` owner. `TURN_LIVE` filters on open
Sessions and released builds record no native input, so PID 29478 could hold a turn or
draft nothing sees; a viewer can reach it, so attaching loses nothing.

Counterexample that stays accepted: an unanswering owner of a completed Session could
hold an unseen draft. Ending it matches idle takeover, which already ends unanswering
superseded released owners whose drafts are equally unrecorded, and only a person's
`lf resume` does it.

Rollover is unchanged. `owner_sessions` returns no open Session, so it stays listed as
"keeps its lf until it exits", which is true and is the rollout's cue; see
[delivery text](#delivery-text).

A SIGSTOPped owner holds SIGTERM until continued, so `end_owner` cannot end one that
stays stopped; its 10 s limit reports that, as on main. The fixture continues the owner
after resume logs the silence.

## Current implementation

- `PendingInput` retains queued bytes and suppresses writable-PTY polling until
  a 100 ms retry deadline. Retention attempts never wait on the store mutex or
  SQLite writer lock; other writers keep their original busy timeout. Changed
  failures log once; recovery resumes record-before-delivery. Poll deadlines round
  up to milliseconds.
- `owner_retention` replaces the boolean-only judgment across retirement,
  resume and rollover. Named facts distinguish native input, Graph, headless
  Run, live turn and no open Session. The watcher also reports output/store
  retention, reuses its connection and limits policy work to the two-second
  poll regardless of activity. Ending-confirmation errors share deduplication.
  An owner held past its detached limit now logs its reason once, however often
  the grace restarts; before, only a changed executable logged, so LOO-711's case
  (unchanged image, held owner) was silent.
- `bind_session_transport` clears `completed_at`: a Session with a live owner is not
  complete, and its next exit retires it again. Before, an ordinary conversation
  reopened after its provider exited kept `completed_at`, so `owner_retention` read
  `NoOpenSession` forever and the owner could never end. This predates the branch.
- Host-security pressure stops later verification suites and retains `not_run`
  receipts. Ordinary product failures still allow independent suites to run.
- `demo.md` creates a durable private root (Machine, provider history, fixture
  repository) and a separate runtime root (copied runtime, generated output) before
  launch, outside temporary directories, with private `LF_HOME` and copied `LF_BIN`
  for launches, child commands and teardown. A person's settled/given-up answer at
  review's end is the release; a headless run settles its own input and releases when
  it ends. Settled input allows verified teardown through `lf resume` and provider
  exit, because no command stops a Session; given-up input leaves owners running.
  `scripts/disk-cleanup.md` names both roots and the marker.

## Demo cleanup decision — settled by accepted decisions, 2026-11-04

The experience is to return to the same editable conversation after a rebuild,
then finish the demo without broken owners or lost history. Session identity
persists; its live owner holds the editor and pending requests. `PendingInput`
is transport machinery, not a new public conversation state.

| Action | Current API and required result |
| --- | --- |
| Type while storage is busy | `PendingInput` → `try_record_owner_input` → PTY: queue until retention commits; keep control responsive. |
| Detach, rebuild, return | `lf resume SESSION` and `owner_retention`: preserve the same editor/request and valid private runtime/store. |
| Finish the demo | Record each draft/request, release the runtime root; `lf resume` and provider exit end verified owners (no command stops a Session), confirmed before it is reclaimed. |
| Resume after teardown | `lf resume SESSION` in the durable private Machine: reopen the same saved history without replay; this cannot recreate a dead editor. |

Earlier reviews held two choices open for demo/review. Jack Heart's own statements
answer both; earlier passes read `2693d72d` for its stop-before-delete policy and
missed what it excludes. This is a reading of his decisions, not a new decision by
him; he can overturn it, and [the owners table](#obligations-and-owners--reconciled-2026-11-04)
says where that reaches him.

1. **History lives outside the disposable runtime.** His accepted brief prefers
   stopping owners and reclaiming disposable demo directories "over indefinite
   retention", preserves durable history, and asks for clean restart from retained
   history after cleanup. Retaining the whole root until teardown cannot meet these
   together: "neither permits deleting history" makes that root permanent.
2. **Unsettled input cannot hold a released root.** `2693d72d`: "Pending drafts or
   quiet-but-live demo owners must not silently retain eligible disposable output
   forever." `demo.md`'s former "Retained or unresolved input keeps its owner and
   complete fixture" contradicted it, as did this plan's former "until disposition".
   Input is recorded and may be lost with its owner. Saved history survives; a dead
   editor does not. Waiting for settlement is not available as a default.

Limits that remain in force: LOO-709's protection still governs idle, replaced and
superseded owners, so its retention decisions do not change. Only authorized
cleanup of a released root ends a draft-holding owner. `4f0b98a7` and `9ad44189` keep
the 24h minimum, so the demo itself never terminates an owner holding unsettled
input: it records the input and releases the root.

Marker: it stays out of the builtin because LOO-722's runner is machine-local and
generic demos do not auto-enroll. Writing it at release restarts the cleaner's
newest-write window, which supplies the 24h minimum; an unknown outcome gets seven
days. Marking at creation would let a long live review age out. `.lf/directions/` was
rejected as its home: prompt directions were removed and nothing reads that directory.

Read against `scripts/disk_cleanup.py` at `6f0d98e016`: marker name, exact
content and `~/.lf-machine/demos/<id>` depth match,
and the marker's mtime enters the window. A demo writes no `.cleanup-evidence.json`,
so every released root is unknown and gets seven days, never 24h; a given-up owner
therefore runs at least that long. The cleaner selects owners by open files under
the runtime root, plus their descendants. With `LF_HOME` moved out, the copied `lf`
