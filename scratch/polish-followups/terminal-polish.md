Jack Heart wants Loopflow's terminal workspaces to feel responsive, legible and intentionally finished during long coding sessions. Copying text, finding an error, moving between panes and returning to waiting work currently provide limited visual feedback; program-selected colors can also disappear into the background. Build a coherent animation, shader and color polish treatment that reduces uncertainty and reading effort while preserving terminal fidelity and fast input.

Why this workflow: the terminal is where Jack repeatedly reads dense output, selects and copies evidence, searches logs, and switches between retained Task contexts. Feedback attached to those concrete actions can confirm what happened without breaking concentration. Cell-level effects belong at the terminal rendering boundary, where selection and color are known; they need a separate treatment from ordinary Swift pane/sidebar styling so rendering costs and semantic color changes can be evaluated together. This is the beneficiary-facing Product outcome even if implementation touches Ghostty.

Scope: cell-level copy/selection/search feedback; restrained focus and state-transition animation; automatic foreground/background contrast treatment; and a consistent policy for motion, shader effects and semantic color across terminal workspaces. Explore mechanisms rather than committing to specific shaders, a theme catalogue, or decorative effects. Keep source colors and any correction distinct so diff, syntax, ANSI, selection, search and reported status remain distinguishable. Respect reduced motion and make effects brief and attributable to a real action; unknown activity must not animate as Working.

Acceptance:
- Demonstrate copy, find, focus and context-return workflows on representative agent CLIs, diffs and a colorful terminal utility; effects make the action/result easier to understand and never cover or alter copied text.
- Demonstrate difficult foreground/background combinations under light and dark terminal palettes, including imported/user colors if supported. Show the chosen correction policy and its limits; preserve meaningful color distinctions and provide an explicit policy for preserving original colors.
- Reduced-motion behavior communicates the same result without relying on animation. Keyboard focus, retained terminal surfaces, drafts, selection, scrolling and provider rendering survive switching and resizing.
- Compare input-to-render and rendering/resource cost against the unchanged baseline in identical scenarios. Keep effects off the input path and state measured limits honestly; no invented timing acceptance.
- Required checks are headless renderer/color/view tests; native visual review assesses whether feedback is helpful and calm. External Ghostty patch/artifact requirements are explicit before delivery.

Relationship: builds on the retained workspaces in Make Loopflow a work-first home for repo and task conversations · LOO-462 (https://linear.app/loopflow/issue/LOO-462/make-loopflow-a-work-first-home-for-repo-and-task-conversations). LOO-462 is separately implementing rounded pane islands, program headers, collapsible Wave/Task navigation, ordinary copy confirmation and unobstructed find UI. Preserve and refine those owners, rather than creating duplicate implementations. Whole-app theme selection/definitions are a separate Task; this Task owns how effects and contrast behave within a chosen palette. No live provider takeover, automatic production installation, or planning migration.
