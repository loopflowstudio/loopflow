Coding agents can do amazing things for you, but they ask a lot of focus from you. How can you, a human with a single locus of attention, stay in sync with an entire parallel agent swarm? Shipping tens of thousands of lines is the easy part. Making them lines worth keeping — lines you don’t revert next month — is the hard part.

Loopflow arranges your agent sessions around the work: long-lived objectives (Waves) and concrete outcomes (Tasks). You don’t have to keep every session window open for fear of it disappearing, or remember why you wanted this massive change in the first place. Each Task follows a workflow you choose, and each session you come to is one where your input really matters. Every Task ends shipped.

Stay engaged and scale the swarm.

A workflow says how a Task gets from an idea to shipped, and where you come in: early on in the design, later on to try the change, both, or neither. Stay close while the work is new, and drop a stop once you trust how it goes. You can zoom in and you can zoom out.

Workflows are files in your repository. Use these three, change them, or write your own, and each project picks the one its Tasks follow, so hands-on work and hands-off work run side by side.

Tasks and Waves ↗Write a workflow ↗

  • feature

    starttask-design →designpursue →demoship →end

    The default. You jam on the design, and you try every change before it ships.
  • code

    startpursue →demoship →end

    One stop. Your agent builds straight from the Task, and you try the change.
  • research

    startresearch →findingsend

    No pull request. Your agent investigates, and the Task ends when you accept the findings.

Underneath, it is all lf: the CLI for software engineering ↗

Free and open source (MIT).

lf has no servers. Everything is done through ephemeral processes (like agent sessions) and ssh. There is telemetry built for you to explore on your own machine, but the maintainers of lf do not receive any of this data in any way. lf can proxy jobs onto remote machines, but everything is done through ssh, not http.

Currently requires claude, codex or opencode, and github. linear is an optional integration. Plays nicely with herdr and cmux.
