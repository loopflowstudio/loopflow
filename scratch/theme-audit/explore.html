Loved themes, and a catalogue built on them

What people pick across editors and terminals, the 24 Loopflow themes as this round leaves them, and a proposal: 26 themes in 13 light-and-dark families, generated from one rule set so every one passes the same measurements.

The answer · Evidence · Map · Compare · Coverage · Limits · Questions · The audit

The answer

  1. Two generations. By total installs the incumbents still lead: GitHub, One Dark, Dracula, Material and Monokai top VS Code and JetBrains. Among people who build their own setup (Neovim configs, GitHub stars, ports) the current favorites are soft pastel families: Catppuccin, Tokyo Night, Kanagawa, Rosé Pine.
  2. Loved themes are families, not files. Catppuccin ships four flavors, Tokyo Night four styles, Rosé Pine three; Solarized, Flexoki and GitHub are one palette in light and dark. You choose the family once and the appearance follows the system.
  3. They follow you between apps. Dracula's organization has 524 repositories of ports and Catppuccin's 362. A terminal-first app is somewhere people expect their theme to show up already.
  4. Almost everything loved is dark. GitHub Light, Atom One Light and Solarized Light are the only light themes in reach of the top lists.
  5. The grounds cluster into a handful of territories: neutral slate (One Dark), navy (Night Owl, Material, Cobalt2), violet-navy (Catppuccin, Rosé Pine, Tokyo Night, Dracula), warm roast (Gruvbox, Kanagawa, Everforest), blue-grey (Nord), neon purple (SynthWave '84, Shades of Purple), paper and ink (Solarized, Flexoki, GitHub), and near-monochrome (Min, zenbones).

The catalogue after this round covers four of those territories well and some twice over (four pale cool lights), and none of slate-with-vivid-syntax or neon. The proposal gives each territory one family in Loopflow's own values, keeps the Loopflow pair exactly as this round ships it, and adds a high-contrast pair. Three more families go where nothing loved is yet, the empty stretches of the map: Verdigris (green-cyan), Fig (magenta, with its hues swapped between light and dark) and Terracotta (red-orange). After this round, the 24 themes have 81 failing measurements between them; the 24 generated themes have 0.

Proposed: 26 in 13 families

Loopflow: the pair as this round ships them, unchanged.

Sorbet: Pastels at one lightness on a violet-navy ground: every accent equally loud, none shouting. Ground held by Catppuccin, Rosé Pine, Tokyo Night.

Signal: Saturated syntax on a neutral slate, the incumbent look: high chroma, clear separation. Ground held by One Dark, Dracula, Monokai.

Harbor: Deep navy with an amber lamp: the blue-black editor ground, warmed by one color. Ground held by Night Owl, Material, Cobalt2, Winter is Coming, Ayu.

Tannin: Roast and parchment with muted earth colors: warm, low glare, made for long sessions. Ground held by Gruvbox, Everforest, Kanagawa, Melange.

Fjord: Calm blue-grey with frost accents: low chroma everywhere, a quiet room. Ground held by Nord.

Folio: Ink on newsprint, and the same inks reversed: one set of accents for both appearances. Ground held by Solarized, Flexoki, GitHub.

Arcade: Neon on midnight, and a risograph print for day: the loud one, kept readable. Ground held by SynthWave '84, Shades of Purple, Andromeda, Cyberdream.

Graphite: Almost no color. Structure by weight, one blue for action, and only an error is loud. Ground held by Min, zenbones, Alabaster, oxocarbon.

Contrast: 7:1 for every word, pure ground, the selection drawn as an outline-strength fill. Ground held by high-contrast modes in macOS, VS Code and JetBrains.

Verdigris: Oxidized copper: a patina-green ground with the bright copper it grew on as the accent. Nothing loved holds a green-cyan ground; the map is empty there.

Fig: The fruit and the leaf: a plum ground with a lime accent at night, a leaf-green ground with plum ink by day. Magenta grounds are empty too, and no loved theme swaps its hues between light and dark.

Terracotta: Fired clay with a cobalt glaze: a burnt-orange ground and the deep blue painted over it. Red-orange grounds are empty: loved warm themes stop at brown.

Limits

  • Install counts measure reach over a decade, not love today. GitHub Theme leads VS Code partly because it is the theme of GitHub; Material's lead in JetBrains and Sublime is from its peak years.
  • Neovim, stars and ports measure people who configure things, which is close to Loopflow's audience but not the same.
  • The families' values are generated from short specs in families.py; nothing is copied from any theme above. They answer the territory a theme holds, not the theme. Someone who wants Catppuccin wants Catppuccin, and Sorbet is not that.
  • Passing the measurements is not looking good. The generator holds contrast and separation; whether Arcade is fun or garish is for eyes.
  • A generated theme can only be as good as the generator's rules. This page uses a generator outside the app; the app's GhosttyThemeMapping has different rules.

Questions

  1. What should the catalogue be?
    1. Loopflow's own families, as proposed: original values, every one passing.
    2. The loved names bundled (Catppuccin, Tokyo Night, Dracula, One Dark, Gruvbox, Nord, Rosé Pine, GitHub; most are MIT), plus the Loopflow pair.
    3. Both: the Loopflow pair and a few families, with the loved names one click away through import.
  2. Should a theme be a family?
    1. Yes: you pick Tannin, and light or dark follows the system.
    2. No: keep two independent pickers, as Choose Theme has now.
  3. Should built-ins be generated from a spec, the way this page's are?
    1. Yes, in the app: a spec of a dozen numbers per theme and one generator, replacing 33 stated roles.
    2. Generate in a script and keep writing full files, as this round did for the defaults.
    3. No: hand-authored files.
  4. Which proposed families are worth keeping? Name them, or "none".
  5. Where does this go?
    1. A new Task after this one lands.
    2. Into this Task now (it is at pull request; this would send it back to build).
    3. Nowhere yet.
