Bundled themes, measured

All 24 themes in swift/Loopflow/Themes/Catalogue/ on the same three screens, with what fails beside each. Nothing in the theme files has changed. Colors are read from the files, value for value; the title bar, selection and diff tints are composited the way the app paints them.

The screens are HTML built for this audit, not the app. They put every role where the app uses it: status words on the sidebar, the selected row, chips, the terminal's 16 colors, added and removed lines, a selected file.

What the catalogue shows

WorkPaletteTests holds in every theme: ordinary text reaches 4.5:1 on every surface and on the neutral selection over it. What it does not measure is where the failures are.

  1. Added and removed lines look alike in 24 of 24 themes. The Diff paints a line with the status chip's fill, and the chip fills were made quiet: at most 7% of the status color over the background. In Loopflow Light the two tints are #ebefe8 and #f5eae6. The plus and minus carry the meaning alone.
  2. A selected file has no fill in 24 of 24. selection-fill is the theme's own surface color at 0.72, so over a surface it changes nothing. Only the 2 pt edge marks the file.
  3. Keyboard focus disappears on the selected row in 21 themes. The ring is accent-ink; on the Work selection fill it is under 3:1. DESIGN.md asks for focus that is visible on a selected row.
  4. Secondary and tertiary text are one color in 5 themes, Loopflow Dark among them. Each must reach 4.5:1 on the surface of the ladder furthest from the ground, and where that surface is mid-tone there is no room for a third level.
  5. 22 of 24 themes are the import mapping's output with an accent and a selection fill chosen by hand; three (Frost, Pewter, Night Owl) are the mapping untouched but for the shadow. Only the two defaults are authored role by role. In 22 themes emphasis ink is plain text, so headings and repo names carry no color.
  6. 10 themes ship ANSI colors under 3:1 on their own terminal background. Loopflow sets Ghostty's minimum-contrast = 3, so the terminal shifts them and shows colors the theme file does not name.
  7. Title bar secondary text is under 4.5:1 in 5 light themes. It is the title bar text at 72% over the accent.
  8. The active pane border is under 3:1 in both defaults (Light 1.9:1, Dark 2.9:1 on the background). The other 22 take it from accent ink and pass.

The two defaults, before and after

Are they one theme?

They share a name, the burgundy button fill #722f37 and the status hues (within 6° of each other, except Available). They do not share a ground or an ink.

Loopflow LightLoopflow Dark
Ground#faf8f5 warm, hue 78°#2b3036 cool slate, hue 253°
Accent ink and emphasis ink (links you press, headings, repo names, focus)#722f37 burgundy#cebfa9 bronze, 62° away
Title bar against the window#722f37 9.1:1#443036 1.09:1, burgundy mixed 65% into the slate by titlebarBackground
Accent button against the window9.1:11.38:1
Burgundy in the terminalANSI magenta is #722f37ANSI magenta is #a67b93, a mauve
Available#5d3539 burgundy family#c8b9c1 chroma 0.020, ΔE 2.8 from secondary text

In Light the burgundy is the title bar, the headings, the focus ring and the button. In Dark it survives as a button fill at 1.38:1 and a title bar at 1.09:1; everything that is burgundy ink in Light is bronze. Cover the name and Dark reads as a slate theme with warm text.

That leaves a choice only you can make: Dark stays the slate-and-bronze it has always been and gets repaired, or Dark becomes Light turned over. Both are drawn below. Light has one proposal, since it is the reference.

What Loopflow can take from it

  1. Generate, don't transcribe. Rex's themes cannot drift from their generator because the roles are never written down per theme. Loopflow already has the generator (GhosttyThemeMapping), and 22 built-ins are its output frozen into files. Every catalogue-wide failure above (same diff tints, invisible file selection, focus on selection) is one rule in the mapping, repeated 22 times. Fixing the rule and regenerating fixes them all; hand-editing 22 files fixes them once.
  2. A status color needs more than ink and one fill. Rex separates the quiet container (a chip) from the solid and from the border. Loopflow asks one fill to be a quiet chip and a diff line, and it can only be one. A container role for diff lines, or a stronger shared fill, is the missing piece.
  3. Selection that works on any surface is translucent. Rex's control states are paints with alpha. Loopflow's file selection is an opaque surface color at a fixed opacity, which is why it vanishes on the surface it was copied from. The proposals above use the text color at 5% for this reason.
  4. The pair shares its hues. Rex's two defaults keep accent and neutral hue fixed and move only lightness. That is Dark B above.
  5. Put the accent in the terminal. Rex ties the accent to the cursor. A burgundy cursor would carry Loopflow's one color into the pane that is the product, in both defaults.
  6. Where Loopflow is stricter, keep the reason in view. Rex lets tertiary text fall to 3.3:1. Loopflow holds 4.5:1, and the price is the collapsed tertiary in five themes. Either the ladder gets shallower (Dark A and B do this) or tertiary is allowed a lower floor for text nobody must read. That is an open question, not a finding.

How it was measured

  • Contrast is WCAG 2 relative luminance, as in WorkPaletteTests: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for marks (focus ring, active border, the plus and minus).
  • Likeness is distance in OKLab ×100. Under 3 is called the same; under 5, close.
  • “Roles the mapping derived” ports GhosttyThemeMapping.palette and counts app roles in the file that equal what an import of the same terminal colors would get.
  • Terminal: every ANSI color against the theme's terminal background, leaving out the color that is the ground by convention (black on dark, white on light).
  • Not measured here: the Dev app itself. The brief's last check, both defaults in the Dev app, comes after a choice.
  • Rebuild: python3 -I scratch/theme-audit/build.py.
