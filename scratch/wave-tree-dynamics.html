Wave tree in motion

Twelve things that happen to work, played through each kind of user, to find where one rule serves everyone and where it doesn't.

Analysis, 2026-11-20. It applies the rules as they stand in scratch/wave-tree.md, including the unanswered ones, so each "what happens" is what the current rules imply, not something decided. Linear behaviour is from scratch/hierarchy-research.md unless marked as from memory.

The three users

  • Solo: a founder, no tracker. Everything travels over Git.
  • Team: five people on one repository with Linear and Slack. The shared tree travels over Git; each person has personal Waves under it.
  • Inside: one person in a large organisation. Every Wave is personal. Some are connected to the organisation's Linear items.

A few people inside a large organisation behave as Team among themselves and as Inside toward everyone else, so they have no column.

What happens

1. You add work to a group: "add these three to store rework"

SoloTeamInside
Three Waves appear under it. Store rework is shared and is a Linear Project, so the three are shared and are filed as issues. Your store-rework Wave is personal, so the three are personal. Nothing is filed.
Lines up, with one rule: a new Wave under a group is shared if the group is shared.

2. You split up the thing you're working on

SoloTeamInside
Waves under yours; your thread hears about them. Personal Waves under your issue. Linear shows the issue and its PRs. The same.
Lines up: a new Wave under a piece of work with a runner is personal.

3. A PR merges on a Wave that still has work under it

SoloTeamInside
The Wave stays open. The Wave stays open. Linear's GitHub integration marks the issue Done if the PR said "Fixes PLAT-212". The same, on an issue your manager is watching.
Tension 1

4. Someone changes the item in Linear: reassigns it, renames it, cancels it

SoloTeamInside
Can't happen. Linear owns those fields, so the Wave shows the change. Cancelled while a runner is working: the thread is told. The same. Reassigned away from you: your Wave and everything under it are now working on someone else's issue.
Lines up on the fields. Tension 2 on what the runner does next

5. Someone moves the issue to another Project in Linear

SoloTeamInside
Can't happen. The shared stretch follows Linear, so the Wave moves under that Project's Wave, with your personal Waves still under it. Your tree is yours. The Wave stays where you put it and shows Linear's Project beside it.
Lines up: Linear moves shared Waves, never personal ones.

Open: the destination Project has no Wave.

6. You move a Wave to a different parent

SoloTeamInside
It moves. A grouping or a personal Wave: it moves. A filed issue to another Project: that is a change in Linear. It moves. Nothing outside changes, even for a connected Wave.
Tension 3

7. A teammate files an issue in the Project, directly in Linear

SoloTeamInside
Can't happen. It shows under the Project's Wave when someone looks. It becomes a Wave when someone starts a thread on it. The same, in your personal view.
Lines up.

The Project's thread only says "two new issues" by itself if something is listening.

8. A teammate files one through Loopflow

SoloTeamInside
Not applicable. Linear has the issue at once. The Wave reaches you over Git about a minute later. In between you see an issue with no Wave. Not applicable.
Lines up if a Wave is identified by its tracker item, so the late Wave attaches to what you already see.

9. An agent learns something worth keeping

SoloTeamInside
Saved to the Wave it was learned in. On close, what matters moves up. Learned in your personal Wave. Moving up on close puts it in shared memory, in front of four other people's agents. All yours, however far it moves.
Tension 4

10. A Wave needs to tell someone something

SoloTeamInside
Goes up to your Discord channel and posts. From your personal Wave, going up finds #platform. Your agent's question about a failing test lands in front of the team. Going up finds #payments, 200 people. It must not post.
Tension 5

11. You connect an existing tree to Linear for the first time

Solo, growing into TeamTeamInside
Billing has forty shared Waves under it. You connect billing to a new Linear Project. The rule "shared under a Project is filed" now owes forty issues. Connecting at setup points at Projects that already hold their issues. Connecting a personal Wave files nothing.
Tension 6

12. A shared group has a job of its own: "every Monday, look at flaky tests"

SoloTeamInside
The root Wave's runner does it on your Machine or your hub. The Wave is shared and has at most one runner. Five people have threads on it. Whose Machine runs Monday's job? Yours.
Tension 7

What lines up

  • Fan-out is personal everywhere. No user wants split-up work filed by default.
  • One default covers "add to a group" and "split my work": under a shared group, shared; under a piece of work with a runner, personal. It gives Team its filing and Inside its silence without a setting.
  • Linear owns title, status and assignee of a connected Wave, for everyone.
  • Linear's moves apply to shared Waves only. Personal trees never rearrange themselves.
  • Unclaimed issues are read when looked at, never mirrored.
  • Reading by going up (settings, memory, context) is safe for all three. Every tension about going up is about writing.

Where it pulls apart

Tension 1. A Wave stays open; its issue closes

You locked that a merge doesn't finish a Wave with work under it, and that a Wave has many PRs over time. Linear closes an issue when a PR that "fixes" it merges. From memory, Linear also accepts wording that links a PR without closing ("Part of", "Ref").

Fits everyone: every PR but the Wave's last links without closing, and closing the Wave closes the issue. The cost is that Loopflow has to know which PR is the last, which is the same as you closing the Wave.

Tension 2. The issue is taken away while work is running

Cancelled or reassigned in Linear, with a runner and personal Waves underneath. Team and Inside both expect to be told, and neither expects Loopflow to stop or discard work on its own. Solo never meets this, so nothing in the solo design covers it.

Tension 3. "Changing the tree changes nothing outside" is false for filed Waves

For Solo and Inside the tree is free. For Team, moving a filed issue between Projects is a Linear write, by the rule that a shared Wave under a Project is that Project's issue. The same command has an outside effect for one user and not the others.

The locked statement has to narrow to: changing the tree changes nothing outside, except for a shared Wave in the tracker's stretch, where a move is a tracker change and says so before it happens.

Tension 4. Memory crossing from personal to shared

Solo wants memory to move up freely; that is how the tree gets smarter. Team wants shared memory to compound too, but a personal Wave's notes arriving in the team's memory unreviewed is the "gunking up" you named. Inside never crosses the line.

The line between personal and shared is where "moves up on close" has to stop and become a choice.

Tension 5. Going up to post

The rule you gave, "if there is no chat attached here, go up until you find one", is right for Solo and wrong for the other two, because for them the first chat found from a personal Wave belongs to other people.

What all three expect: a personal Wave reaches you. A shared Wave may post where its team reads. That needs two things going up finds separately, where to reach me and where this group talks, or a rule that posting from a personal Wave stops at the line.

Tension 6. Connecting can create

You said connecting attaches and creating is deliberate. But "shared under a Project is filed" means connecting a group that already has shared Waves owes Linear an issue for each. This is exactly the solo founder graduating to a team, which is the path the levels are supposed to make smooth.

Tension 7. A shared Wave's runner

"At most one runner" and "agents are not shared between people" are both locked. A shared grouping Wave with a standing job needs a runner that is nobody's in particular. With no server several people share, it runs on one person's Machine by arrangement. This is the first place the open-source line costs Team something concrete.

Questions

  1. PRs and the Linear issue: a. every PR but the last links without closing, and closing the Wave closes the issue. b. the first merge closes the issue and the Wave lives on beyond it. c. each PR that needs to be seen gets its own issue.
  2. The issue is cancelled or reassigned while a runner works: a. tell the thread and keep going. b. pause the runner and tell the thread. c. keep going for cancelled-by-you, pause for anyone else's change.
  3. Moving a filed Wave between Projects in Loopflow: a. changes Linear, after saying so. b. isn't allowed; move it in Linear. c. moves in Loopflow only and shows a mismatch.
  4. Memory from a personal Wave closing under a shared one: a. moves into shared memory. b. moves into your personal memory at that level; you publish entries. c. the closing step proposes what to share and you approve.
  5. A personal Wave that needs a person: a. reaches only you, wherever you are reachable. b. posts in the nearest channel above. c. a setting on the connection.
  6. Connecting a group with shared Waves under it to a Project: a. lists what would be filed and asks. b. files them. c. connects and makes the existing ones personal to whoever made them.
  7. A shared Wave's standing job: a. runs on one named person's Machine. b. isn't offered without a shared server. c. any member's Machine may pick it up, one at a time.
